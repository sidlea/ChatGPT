### 2.4 Kitchen SOPs: hero items

**SOP-01 Tzatziki** (makes about 1.4 kg; label with a 48-hour use-by)
1. Grate 600 g cucumber coarsely, seeds removed. Mix with 6 g salt and rest 15 minutes.
2. **Squeeze hard in a clean cloth** until no liquid drips, leaving about 300 g. This fixes "watery".
3. Mix in 1 kg **strained Greek yoghurt (10% fat)**, 12–15 g garlic grated on a microplane (no chunks), 30 ml lemon juice, 40 ml extra virgin olive oil and 8–10 g chopped dill or mint. Season.
4. Rest 1 hour chilled. **Pass check:** a spoon stands up, there is no pool of whey, and there are no visible cucumber chunks.

**SOP-02 Gyros chicken thigh, "gyros loaf" method** (5 kg batch; fast at service and consistent)
1. Marinade per 5 kg boneless thigh: 500 g Greek yoghurt, 100 ml olive oil, 100 ml lemon juice, 60 g garlic, 25 g dried oregano, 25 g smoked paprika, 10 g cumin, 10 g black pepper, **50 g salt (1% of meat weight)**, optional 5 g cinnamon. Marinate 12–24 hours.
2. Pack the thighs tightly into lined, deep gastronorm trays, press and cover. Roast at 160°C to a **75°C core**, probed and recorded.
3. Chill fast (blast or ice bath; below 8°C within 90 minutes is good practice **[verify against your SFBB]**), then label.
4. **To order:** slice 5 mm thick across the loaf and crisp on a hot plancha for 60–90 seconds per side. Serve at 75°C or above when reheated (the Safer Food Better Business standard), or hot-hold at 63°C or above.

**SOP-03 Skin-on chips** (25 kg sack, about 100 portions)
1. Wash with skins on and cut to 12 mm.
2. Rinse until the water runs clear, then dry.
3. Blanch at 140°C for 6–8 minutes until soft and not coloured. Tray, cool and hold chilled for up to 24 hours.
4. To order, fry at 180°C for 3–4 minutes until golden.
5. Portion with a scoop: **180 g cooked**.

**SOP-04 Pizza**
- **If making dough in-house:** 1 kg 00 pizza flour, 620 ml cold water (62%), 25 g salt, 2 g fresh yeast. Mix, bulk-ferment 2 hours, ball at **250 g**, cold-ferment 24–48 hours, and temper for 2 hours before service.
- **Topping weights:** 80 g sauce and **110 g cheese** (pre-weighed into portion tubs).
- **Bake** to the oven's spec; the leopard-spotting or colour target goes on the spec card.
- **Pass check:** an even rim, no pooled water, and the cheese fully melted but not split or oily.

**SOP-05 Smash burger**
- Use 2 × 90 g balls of 80/20 chuck mince from the butcher, pre-portioned and kept chilled.
- Smash on a very hot plate with greaseproof, season, flip at a deep crust, and add American cheese.
- Toasted brioche, house pickles and burger sauce, in a fixed build order.
- **Core temperature 75°C**, or follow your documented HACCP if you serve differently **[verify with EHO]**.

**SOP-06 Seasoning control** (fixes Cassiel's "salty soup")
- Every soup, sauce and dressing recipe states salt in grams per kg.
- **Pre-service tasting line-up:** the chef on duty tastes every sauce, soup, dressing and marinade before service and initials the sheet.
- Nothing is "seasoned to taste" without a target.

**Standard kitchen SOP pack**, each on one page and laminated:
- Opening and closing checks, and temperature logs (fridges at 5°C or below is best practice; 8°C is the legal maximum).
- Delivery receiving: check temperature, weight, use-by and the product code against the allergen matrix.
- Day-dot labelling and FIFO.
- Par-level prep lists by day.
- Batch cooking, cooling and reheating.
- Hot holding at 63°C or above.
- Allergen controls (§6).
- **Foreign-body controls**, relevant to the January 2026 pasta complaint: blue plasters, hair coverings, no glass in the kitchen, a damaged-equipment check, and a complaint and incident log. These records also support a "due diligence" defence under the Food Safety Act 1990 **[verify with your solicitor]**.
- Waste log and pass checks.

**Photo method:**
- Shoot one perfect plate, approved by the head chef.
- Mount the phone on a fixed tripod and take one overhead shot and one at 45°.
- Use the same plate, light and background every time, with a spoon or ruler in the corner for scale.
- File it as the dish code plus version.
- Reshoot whenever the spec changes.

### 2.6 Pass checks: the 8-second gate
The head chef or sous chef "owns the pass" at every service. Nothing leaves without passing all of these:
1. **Right table and right dish**, with modifiers and **allergen flag** checked against the ticket.
2. **Looks like the photo.** Compare with the spec card.
3. **Portion** looks right. Run a weight spot-check twice per service on chips, cheese and protein.
4. **Temperature:** the plate is hot and the food is hot. Probe chicken and burgers at least twice per service and log it.
5. **Seasoning and sauce** consistency. Tzatziki must not be watery.
6. **Clean rim**, garnish correct, nothing missing.
7. **Ticket time:** if a ticket has gone over 20 minutes for a main, tell the floor manager so they can communicate with the table.
8. **The value question: "Would I pay £15.95 for this?"** If not, it doesn't go.

**Theoretical vs actual.** Theoretical dish GP of 75–80% leaves room for waste, staff food, comps and promotions while still landing an **actual 65–70%**. If actual GP is more than **3 points below theoretical**, the cause is portioning, waste, unrecorded transfers or theft (see §5).

## 3. Menu engineering and pricing method

### 3.1 The method: Kasavana & Smith menu engineering
Classify each item **within its category** (starters, pizza, mains and so on) on two tests ([beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/08/menu-engineering-food-cost-percentage-stars-plowhorses-puzzles-dogs-matrix); [RobotPOS](https://en.robotpos.com/restaurant-menu-engineering); [QSR Web](https://www.qsrweb.com/blogs/making-qsr-menus-maximize-profits/)):
- **Popularity:** the item's menu-mix % is at least **70% × (1 ÷ number of items in the category)**.
- **Profitability:** the item's **contribution margin in £** (net price − plate cost) is at least the category's **weighted average CM**. Use £ contribution, not GP%, because £ is what pays the rent.

| | High CM | Low CM |
|---|---|---|
| **High popularity** | **Star**: protect the spec, feature it, don't discount | **Plowhorse**: nudge the price up £0.25–£0.75, trim cost without guests noticing, bundle with high-GP drinks |
| **Low popularity** | **Puzzle**: reposition (top of section, box, better name or description, server recommendation, photo on social), or simplify | **Dog**: remove, unless strategic (e.g. the only vegan main), then rework |

**Target price formula:** `Price incl. VAT = plate cost ÷ (1 − target GP) × 1.2`.
For example, a £3.00 plate cost at 72% target GP gives £3.00 ÷ 0.28 = £10.71 net, or £12.86 gross. Round to **£12.95**. Then sanity-check against competitor and value perception.

### 3.4 Pricing psychology (use sparingly and honestly)
- **Anchor high.** A £42 platter for two and a £23.95 steak make £14–16 plates feel fair.
- **Decoys and ladders:**
  - Burgers: single £11.95 / **double £14.95** / triple £17.95. The double becomes the default.
  - Cocktails: Classics £8.50 / **Signatures £9.50** / Reserve £12.50. Most guests choose the middle tier, so make it your best margin.
  - Wine: make the **second tier** your best-margin bottle **[evidence for "second-cheapest" behaviour is mixed; consultant view]**.
- **Remove £ signs, dotted leaders and price columns.** In the Cornell study, diners given menus without currency signs spent about 8% more ([Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/node/273666)). It was a single-site study, so treat it as directional.
- **Keep price endings consistent** (.50 / .95). Avoid .99 at casual-premium.
- **Bundles only for slow periods:** "Pizza + pint £15 Mon–Thu", "Gig night: pizza & 2 drinks", "Sunday kleftiko for 2". Every bundle must pass the worst-case GP rule (≥65%).
- **Small measures are a compliance point.** Mandatory licensing conditions require 25/35 ml spirits, 125 ml wine and half pints to be available and made known to customers **[verify wording with your licensing adviser]**.

### 3.5 Layout and portion control
**Layout:**
- One A4 or A3 card per venue, with a matching QR and Google Business Profile menu. Google is currently flagging that no menu is uploaded.
- 4–7 items per section. Put the **star at the top of each section** and box one item per section.
- Short descriptions with provenance ("our butcher's Yorkshire beef") that are **accurate**.
- Allergen and dietary icons (V, VG, GF-available) plus "Please tell us about allergies; ask for our allergen guide".

**Portion control:**
- Scales at each section.
- Colour-coded ladles and portion pots.
- Proteins pre-portioned and labelled at prep.
- Cheese pre-weighed into tubs per pizza.
- One chip scoop size.
- Weekly random weight audit of five plates.
